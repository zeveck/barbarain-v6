# BarbarAIn — Creative Brief

**In one sentence:** A modern, button-driven text adventure RPG in the Hyborian Age of Robert E. Howard, where a broke Cimmerian thief-barbarian pulls one heist in Shadizar and ends up fighting across time, sea and dimensions to stop a cult from waking a dragon that lives between worlds.

**The feeling:** Pulp sword-and-sorcery: sweat, steel, lamplight and gold; terse, grim, funny heroes; dread sorcery; and the delight of a clever puzzle solved with a stolen item and a bad idea.

## What they asked for

- [ ] Modern-leaning text adventure: describes scenes, lets you explore
- [ ] Explore dungeons
- [ ] Fight monsters
- [ ] Gain levels, HP and MP, magic
- [ ] A range of weapons that the characters use
- [ ] Typical RPG elements
- [ ] Simple 16-bit style graphics: environments, towns, people, the hero's party, enemies
- [ ] Interface playable on desktop and mobile
- [ ] Called **BarbarAIn**
- [ ] Heavily based on the world of Conan the Barbarian (Robert E. Howard's books and stories)
- [ ] Characters evoking those from the books and movies, behaving "in character"
- [ ] A new story
- [ ] At least three major twists/surprises that make sense in the world
- [ ] Loose ideas offered: robots from the future, time travel, pirates, trans-dimensional dragons, a heist ("anything goes", "sure, if you can make it fit")
- [ ] Light adventure-game puzzles à la Secret of Monkey Island
- [ ] Items, armor and weapons with good quality-of-life inventory management
- [ ] More than one ending, "say three" (loose on the number, firm on >1)
- [ ] At least one ending only opens because of a mid-story choice
- [ ] A few optional side quests, "maybe three" (loose on the number)
- [ ] One side quest is an arena in Shadizar where you fight your way up
- [ ] Big: a couple of hours, "five or so" chapters, lots to explore

## Who, where, how long

Adults and teens who like classic RPGs, adventure games and pulp fantasy. Desktop (mouse + keyboard, optional typed commands) and phones (portrait, thumb-friendly buttons). Sessions of 10–40 minutes; the whole game about two hours; autosave so a phone session can stop anywhere.

## Style references

- **Robert E. Howard's Conan stories** (The Tower of the Elephant, Queen of the Black Coast, Red Nails, The Hour of the Dragon, Rogues in the House): borrow the Hyborian Age geography (Shadizar, Zamora, Argos/Messantia, Stygia, Cimmeria), its gods (Crom, Mitra, Set), its tone (laconic heroes, decadent cities, ancient alien horrors, sorcery as corrupting), and its archetypes (the Zamorian thief, the pirate queen, the serpent-priest, the alien being in the tower). Avoid using Conan himself, or lifting any story's plot or text.
- **The 1982 film**: borrow the mood (big, operatic, mythic), the archetypes (a grumpy wizard companion, a charismatic serpent cult leader). Avoid film-only characters, names, lines and imagery.
- **16-bit console RPGs / adventure games** (SNES-era JRPGs, early-90s point-and-click): borrow the front-view turn-based battles over a painted scene, the chunky pixel scenes and portraits, verb-on-object puzzles and comic dialogue choices. Don't copy any game's characters, UI art or puzzles (no insult sword-fighting).

## Settings and decisions made for the player

- Platform both, web target, large scope, $20 budget, 2 review rounds.
- **The hero is a new Cimmerian, Brannoc, not Conan.** Conan himself is a trademarked character, and the request asks for a new story; every character is original but evokes a Howard/film archetype and talks like one.
- Real Howard setting names and gods are used (Shadizar, Zamora, Argos, Stygia, Crom, Set, Mitra).
- **DOM interface, not a canvas game**: text, buttons and pixel-art scene pictures, so it reads well on a phone.
- The twists use the loose ideas (heist, robots from the future, pirates, time travel, a trans-dimensional dragon) and tie them together so each one makes sense in the Hyborian Age.
